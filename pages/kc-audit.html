Audit Types

Types of Audit in India

Every audit type serves a distinct purpose, understanding which applies to your business is the first step to compliance.

⚖️

Statutory Audit

Mandatory under Companies Act 2013 for all registered companies. An independent CA examines financial statements and expresses an opinion on whether they present a true and fair view. Governed by Standards on Auditing issued by ICAI.

📋

Tax Audit (Section 44AB)

Mandatory for businesses with turnover exceeding ₹1 crore (₹10 crore if 95%+ digital) and professionals with gross receipts above ₹50 lakh. CA certifies Form 3CA/3CB and the detailed Form 3CD. Due by 30th September.

🔍

Internal Audit

Mandatory under Section 138 for listed companies, unlisted public companies above specified thresholds, and private companies with turnover above ₹200 crore or outstanding loans above ₹100 crore. Risk-based; focuses on operational controls and governance.

✅

Compliance Audit

A multi-law review covering Companies Act, GST, Income Tax, Labour Laws, FEMA, and RERA obligations. Identifies compliance gaps before regulators do. Often paired with secretarial audit (Form MR-3) for listed and specified unlisted companies.

📦

Stock Audit

Physical verification of inventories for banks, NBFCs, or management purposes. Certifies stock quantities, valuation, and quality. Mandatory for entities with working capital credit limits above bank-prescribed thresholds. Conducted periodically by empanelled CAs.

🏦

Bank / Concurrent Audit

Audit of banking operations, financial statements, and regulatory compliance. Concurrent audit runs alongside daily bank operations to ensure real-time transaction accuracy, limit compliance, and KYC adherence as per RBI guidelines.

🏛️

Government / PSU Audit

Audit of government entities, public sector undertakings, NGOs receiving grants, and cooperative societies. Follows CAG-prescribed standards. Focuses on fund utilisation, tendering compliance, and efficiency of government expenditure.

💰

Cost Audit

Mandatory for companies in specified industries (cement, pharma, telecom, etc.) with turnover above prescribed limits. CA (Cost) or CMA certifies cost records in Form CRA-3. Ensures cost accounting records are maintained correctly.

🔐

Information Systems Audit

Review of IT systems, cybersecurity controls, data integrity, and access management. Increasingly relevant as financial reporting becomes system-dependent. Important for banks, NBFCs, large corporates, and digital businesses.

CARO 2020

Companies Auditor's Report Order 2020

CARO 2020 replaced CARO 2016 and significantly expanded auditor reporting obligations. It applies to all companies except certain small private companies, banking companies, insurance companies, and Section 8 companies below threshold.

CARO requires auditors to report on 21 specific clauses covering fixed assets, inventories, loans, investments, statutory dues, borrowings, related-party transactions, fraud, and CSR, among others.

❖
Fixed Assets (Clause 1)
Whether the company maintains proper records of fixed assets, conducts physical verification periodically, and reports any material discrepancies found.
❖
Loans to Related Parties (Clause 4)
Compliance with Section 185 and 186. Auditor must report on terms and recoverability. Loans not at arm's length must be specifically flagged.
❖
Statutory Dues (Clause 7)
Report whether GST, PF, ESIC, TDS, customs, and income tax dues are regularly deposited. Amounts outstanding over 6 months must be specifically disclosed.
❖
Fraud Reporting (Clause 11)
Mandatory reporting of fraud by or on the company. Fraud above ₹1 crore must be reported to the Central Government. Cash losses in current and prior year must be disclosed.

"CARO 2020 transformed the auditor's role from a financial statement verifier to a governance watchdog, requiring detailed reporting on fraud, related-party transactions, whistle-blowers, and CSR compliance."

V J Desai & Co. LLP, Audit Advisory
❖
Whistle-Blower Complaints (Clause 18)
Auditors must consider complaints received under the vigil mechanism (Section 177) and report on how management has handled them during the year.
❖
CSR Compliance (Clause 19)
Whether the company has complied with Section 135 CSR provisions, including whether unspent CSR amounts have been transferred to prescribed funds within deadlines.
❖
Undisclosed Transactions (Clause 1(vii))
Auditors must report on transactions not recorded in the books and whether they have been properly accounted for in the financial statements.
❖
Internal Financial Controls (Clause 1(vi))
Whether the company has adequate IFC with reference to financial statements, and whether such controls operated effectively during the year.

Standards on Auditing

Key ICAI Standards on Auditing

Standards on Auditing (SAs) issued by ICAI govern how audits must be planned, executed, and reported in India.

📋

SA 200, Overall Objectives

Defines the auditor's overall objectives: obtaining reasonable assurance that financial statements are free from material misstatement. Establishes the foundation of professional scepticism and audit judgment.

🔍

SA 315, Risk Assessment

Requires the auditor to understand the entity, its environment, and internal controls to identify and assess risks of material misstatement at both the financial statement and assertion levels.

🛡️

SA 240, Fraud Responsibilities

Addresses the auditor's responsibilities relating to fraud. Requires professional scepticism, specific fraud risk inquiries, and defined procedures when fraud risk factors are identified.

⚠️

SA 570, Going Concern

Requires the auditor to evaluate management's going concern assessment. If material uncertainty exists, the auditor must report it, even if the financials are otherwise fairly presented.

📊

SA 540, Accounting Estimates

Governs auditing of accounting estimates and fair value disclosures. Requires evaluation of management's estimation process, inputs used, and reasonableness of assumptions applied.

📄

SA 700, Forming an Opinion

Governs the form and content of the auditor's report on complete general-purpose financial statements. Specifies the required elements of an unmodified (clean) audit opinion.

✏️

SA 705, Modified Opinions

Covers circumstances where the auditor issues a qualified opinion, adverse opinion, or disclaimer of opinion, including specific language and placement of modifications in the audit report.

🤝

SA 600, Using Work of Others

Governs group audit engagements involving component auditors. The group auditor retains full responsibility for the group audit opinion and must oversee component auditor work appropriately.

🔒

SA 505, External Confirmations

Governs the use of external confirmation procedures, including bank confirmations, debtor confirmations, and legal representations. Covers design, execution, and evaluation of responses.

NFRA Inspection Reports

NFRA has published inspection reports highlighting deficiencies in documentation, going concern evaluation, and estimates auditing across CA firms.

  • Adequate working paper documentation
  • Going concern assessment rigor
  • Accounting estimate scrutiny
  • Professional scepticism application

IFC Reporting

Internal Financial Controls

Revised ICAI guidance on IFC reporting under CARO 2020, auditors must assess design adequacy AND operating effectiveness of controls.

  • Separate IFC report for listed companies
  • Control testing, design and operation
  • IT controls increasingly important
  • Management responsibility assertion

Digital Audit

Technology in Audit

ICAI guidance on use of data analytics, AI tools, and automated testing in audit engagements. Auditors must understand IT environments and assess data integrity risks.

  • CAAT, Computer Assisted Audit Techniques
  • ERP audit trail verification
  • Access control and segregation review
  • Cybersecurity risk in audit scope
